> The rename exposed something a rename cannot fix. The plate device was built
> around **W**, one of the widest and densest capitals: at cap 44 on the 66-unit
> plate it advances 45.09, leaving 10.46 each side. Archivo's **T** advances
> 29.38 — 18.31 each side — so the letter sits in the plate instead of filling
> it, which three independent reviews flagged. It cannot be scaled out of:
> matching the W's width needs cap ≈ 72 on a 66-unit plate.
>
> The resolution keeps the concept and changes the draughtsmanship. **A
> flat-faced (mushroom) tappet in profile is already a T** — a wide flat crown
> where the cam lobe strikes, on a narrower cylindrical body — so the letter and
> the part the product is named after are one silhouette. It fills 68.3% of the
> plate: the W's optical box, exactly.
>
> ⚠ **This changes B1 and the one-drawing rule, and both are amended below
> rather than quietly broken.** The mark's letter is no longer set in the
> wordmark's typeface. The wordmark itself is unchanged and still Archivo.
>
> ⚠ Measured, so the record is not overstated in the other direction: the
> typeset T did **not** fail at small sizes — its 16px favicon rendered a clean
> 2px stem, 5.9% antialiased. The drawn one renders a **4px body**, so it is
> more robust at 16px, not less. This was a proportion decision, not a rescue.

## Aesthetic direction (given to the critic)

Well Kept keeps a car's service record: it reads invoices, anchors maintenance
intervals, surfaces recalls, and gives an owner a defensible picture of their car's
condition. Its standing product position is **ranges over verdicts, "unknown" over a
guessed default** — it never claims more than the data supports, and the identity should
feel like an instrument that reports rather than a brand that promises. The house
register is the original Fast & Furious photographic language revived for 2026: night,
wet asphalt, sodium streetlight against cold cyan, matte graphite near-black; **two hues
only, used as light rather than as fill**; panel-cut chamfered geometry; a condensed
grotesk display face with mono for data.

## The studio version

Concept: **the data plate.** Every car carries one stamped plate that *is* its record —
the VIN plate on the door jamb. Tappet's mark is that plate in the house geometry, and
its letter is stamped, not printed.

The mark is a solid chamfered plate with a single **T cut clean through it** — whatever
sits behind the plate shows through the letter. That T is **drawn, not typeset**: it is a
flat-faced tappet in profile, whose crown-and-body silhouette is a T, with a machined
fillet where the crown meets the body. The fillet is the tell — a square armpit reads as
a letterform, a radius reads as a turned steel part. That one rule yields both polarities from
one drawing: off-white plate on graphite, graphite plate on light, and on a photograph the
wet asphalt shows through the T. No stroke, no glow, no rivets, no monogram.

⚠ **Amended 7 Sep:** this line previously read "the T is the same glyph as the wordmark's
T, so mark and wordmark are one drawing." That is no longer true and was traded
deliberately. The mark's letter is drawn to the plate's optical box; the wordmark stays
typeset Archivo. They share weight, width and the house geometry — not outlines. What is
bought is a plate the letter actually fills, and a mark that is bespoke rather than a
capital in a box, which is worth something to a product that has lost two names to
trademark collisions.

The wordmark stands free, never boxed: TAPPET in the condensed heavy grotesk, caps,
tracking −1%, one word. Maker line SOUTHMOOR DIGITAL in the mono, caps, +10%
tracking, ~30% of wordmark cap height, flush left beneath. Short lockup: plate-mark beside
wordmark, baseline-aligned, gap of half the mark's width.

App icon: matte graphite tile; plate ~66% of tile width, centred, off-white; identical
drawing at every size. Nav: mark plus wordmark at ≥20px cap height, within 140px.

The mark carries **no hue**. The two hues are light, and light belongs to the environment
— the photograph, and UI states. A glowing badge promises; a stamped plate reports.

## Brief checklist

- **B1.** *(amended 7 Sep)* **Wordmark** set in the north star's condensed heavy grotesk;
  no serif anywhere in it. The **mark's** letter is drawn to the plate's optical box —
  a tappet in profile — matched to the wordmark in weight and width, not in outline.
- **B2.** Mark is a solid chamfered plate with the T cut through it; no stroke, glow or rivets.
  The crown-to-body fillet is part of the drawing and survives to 16px.
- **B3.** One drawing at 1024/180/120/87/60/40/29/16 — T only, no polarity flip, no monogram→letter switch.
  ⚠ "One drawing" now means one *mark*, at every size. It no longer means the mark and
  the wordmark share an outline — see the amendment above.
- **B4.** Off-white plate on dark, graphite plate on light, same file; mono-on-light at 24px shows a readable T.
- **B5.** No hue inside the mark; cyan and sodium appear only in photography and UI states.
- **B6.** Wordmark stands free, never boxed; nav lockup fits 140px at ≥20px cap height.
- **B7.** Maker line in the mono, caps, letterspaced, flush left under the wordmark.
- **B8.** On a photograph the mark sits over asphalt, not the car, and the photo shows through the T.
- **B9.** Home-screen tile is matte graphite #1A1A1A with the plate ~66% of tile width.
